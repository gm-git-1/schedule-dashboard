Our Smaller Model Beat Opus on Finance Tasks - Alexander Shevchenko, Ramp

AI Engineer · Alexander Shevchenko, Ramp (Ramp Labs applied AI) · uploaded 5 Nov, 3:30 AM IST · 14:15
Verdict: worth watching — short, with three concrete production techniques (specialist model, cartridges, cheap-model swarms) rather than slogans.
🔁 Repeat: 'AI for finance' — seen 22 times before, 'token costs / AI spend' 19 times, 'RL fine-tuning small models' 6 times and 'AI-found vulnerabilities' 4 times. New here: KV-cache "cartridges" prepared overnight for month-end close, a 10,000-agent pen-test swarm where open-weight models were up to 5x cheaper, and transferring a LoRA post-train across model versions.
Why an app company does research
  • Models keep getting cheaper: LLM use cases at Ramp are up 123% in a year, and top features spend more tokens overall but are cheaper per unit because each new frontier model is cheaper, faster and better (0:00–1:00)
  • Why not just wait: In 2023 GPT-4 agents needed strict guardrails, couldn't backtrack and broke on synonyms, but Ramp shipped anyway (Ramp Tour Guide); agents are easy now, so the edge comes from digging a layer deeper (1:00–3:00)
Three research bets sent to production
  • Specialist model beats Opus: Ramp Sheets (launched last November) spent ages planning and reading every sheet with slow, expensive Opus 4.6, so with Prime Intellect they post-trained a Qwen 3.5 35B financial question-answering model that beat Opus 4.6 on exact task accuracy at far lower latency, with the frontier model kept as orchestrator (3:00–6:00)
  • Cartridges (KV-cache compression): Compresses 20–30x more context into the KV cache offline, so for month-end close the ledger, aging report and documents can be pre-built overnight and the agent already "knows" them when the accountant opens it on the 1st; they'd welcome labs shipping this in their APIs (6:00–8:00)
  • 10,000-agent pen test: Using their cloud agent Ramp Inspect, they ran 10,000 parallel agents over all of Ramp, de-duplicated and adversarially reproduced findings with LLMs, and patched 7 high-severity bugs, including a Lua script injection via email that no single component exposed; open-weight models up to 5x cheaper gave nearly the same detection rate in a swarm (8:00–10:00)
Products and other research
  • Token spend management and Ramp Router: A product that maps LLM spend by key, user, accounting category and even tax credits with a semantic usage taxonomy, plus a router using strategies like Nvidia Switchyard and flex offloading to cut latency and cost (pitch) (10:00–12:00)
  • Ramp Labs blog extras: "Portal" transfers a LoRA post-train to newer Qwen versions or other model families, self-improving Datadog monitors that adjust without a human, and open-ended semantic tagging of user sessions (12:00–14:00)

AI Labs Will Trap You or Replace You - Dillon Rolnick, Nous Research

AI Engineer · Dillon Rolnick, Nous Research (CEO) · uploaded 5 Nov, 2:30 AM IST · 15:13
Verdict: skip — an opinion piece and Hermes Agent pitch; skim 2:00–6:00 if you want the lock-in argument.
🔁 Repeat: 'open source / local models' — seen 51 times before and 'vendor lock-in' 5 times. New here: mostly an argument rather than data; the fresh bits are the claim that labs must either lock in businesses or compete with them directly (finance first), and that Nous cloned xAI's Grok bots UI into Hermes Agent in a weekend.
The labs' incentive problem
  • Models are commoditizing: Practitioners switch freely between Astra, Fable, Opus and Kimi, and per-token prices fall while total token spend surges and serving costs stay flat, so margins on frontier models are narrowing (1:00–2:00)
  • Two ways to earn back trillions: Move to another layer (infrastructure or the user, hence the fight at the agent layer), or "build God" and monetize it; businesses, not fickle consumers, are where the token money is (2:00–4:00)
  • Trap or replace: If ~70% of a company's output is agentic in 2–3 years and a lab controls those agents, it can change the rules and raise prices; or labs point their best models at lucrative problems like finance themselves, starting by hiring bankers and partnering with big firms (opinion) (3:00–6:00)
Nous's counter-pitch
  • Software moats are gone: Nous pointed Hermes Agent at xAI's Grok bots interface and reproduced it as an open-source Hermes feature in a weekend; Rolnick says future moats are network effects, reputation and reliability (8:00–10:00)
  • Distillation framing: Labs call teaching one model from another "illicit distillation" and train models to refuse to help train other models, labelling that safety, which he calls self-serving (repeat) (9:00–11:00)
  • Agents are operating systems: Agents combine deterministic code, data sources and model judgment like a framework, so build on a base where you can swap models and inherit improvements rather than maintain your own agent stack (11:00–14:00)
  • Hermes Agent claim: He says Hermes Agent is the most-used and most-downloaded open-source agent, an order of magnitude ahead of close competitors (no figures shown) (14:00–15:13)

Robots Will Be Trained on Video Games - Pim de Witte, General Intuition

AI Engineer · Pim de Witte, General Intuition (and Medal) · uploaded 5 Nov, 1:30 AM IST · 18:51
Verdict: worth watching — a genuinely different take on robotics and computer use, though no demo video is shown.
🔁 Repeat: 'training data pipelines' — seen 5 times before. New here: nearly all of it; robotics and world models have come up only once before, and game recordings as robot training data is new.
The idea
  • Act while observing: Using Olaf from Frozen reassembling mid-roll as the example, de Witte argues turn-based next-token LLMs can't handle space and time, so purpose-built "pixels in, actions out" models fit robotics better (0:00–2:00)
  • Games share robots' action space: Medal, the video-game recorder, captures tens of thousands of games with controller inputs tied to frames; since many robots below a certain number of degrees of freedom ship with a game controller, the same actions transfer to teleoperation (1:00–4:00)
  • Skip predicting motor torque: Rather than predicting poses or motor values end-to-end like most robotics firms, the model outputs keyboard, mouse or controller actions, essentially an enhanced computer-use model, and the controller handles the physics (4:00–7:00)
The data bet
  • Scale: Medal gets about 1 billion uploads a year, on track for 3 billion, giving over 1.5 trillion action tokens a month; he says they collect in half a day what frontier labs use in total to train robotics models (6:00–7:00)
  • Action density: Gamers make far more actions per minute than teleoperators, which matters for jobs like drone flying with many small corrections; the model also learns the meaning of each action, helping interpretability (8:00–10:00)
  • Versus V-JEPA: Meta's JEPA infers action labels instead of training on them; he calls it more correct in the limit but less practical, and says real action data (like rudder moves invisible in video) is their moat (17:00–18:51)
Release and uses
  • One model, many bodies: One model plays games at top level and controls over 10 robot types in real time; early access over the next few months, release planned late November or early December, with hackathons in New York (mid-November) and London (10:00–17:00)
  • Use cases: Meant to sit under an orchestrator like Gemini or Astra for planning; ideas include exploring generated environments, UX testing as a human would, robot science labs, and accessibility (e.g. one-armed users playing games while the model handles the keyboard) (11:00–15:00)

Don't Approve the AI Model. Approve the App - Doneyli De Jesus, Langfuse

AI Engineer · Doneyli De Jesus, Langfuse (by ClickHouse) · uploaded 4 Nov, 3:30 PM IST · 19:20
Verdict: skim from 7:00 — the calculator-tool fix and compliance-gate example are the useful parts; the rest restates familiar eval advice.
🔁 Repeat: 'evals for agents' — seen 54 times before, 'agent observability' 30 times, 'human-in-the-loop review' 29 times, 'AI in regulated industries' 23 times, 'AI for finance' 22 times and 'LLM-as-a-judge' 11 times. New here: the top-5 bank numbers (97% groundedness but 60% numeric accuracy, fixed to about 95% with a calculator tool) and a binary regulatory-compliance gate that can block release on its own.
What the bank wanted
  • Certify apps, not models (repeat): The bank ignored public benchmarks; for a 10-K analyst for investment bankers, it wanted proof the application works on its own data, saying "we don't certify models, we certify applications" (0:00–4:00)
  • On-prem, gated, air-gapped: Banks are reopening data centers and standing up GPUs to run models locally, refuse even zero-retention API calls, and need air-gapped deployments (3:00–5:00)
The gating method
  • Golden dataset as test cases (repeat): Inputs, expected outputs and metadata are run against the agent; traces become evidence and scores give pass or fail per dimension, re-run whenever the prompt, model or tools change (5:00–11:00)
  • 97% grounded, 60% on numbers: A model grounded 97% of answers in bank data but got financial numbers right only about 60% of the time; giving it a deterministic Python calculation tool lifted numeric accuracy to about 95% and cleared the bank's 85% gate (7:00–13:00)
  • Mix scorers: Deterministic code checks, checks on the path the agent took, and LLM judges for qualitative parts, since LLM judges alone "won't cut it" (11:00–13:00)
  • One dimension can block release: Across 150- and 1,500-question datasets, a config scored well on groundedness and completeness but zero on binary regulatory compliance, so averages hide it; risk experts set per-use-case thresholds (13:00–16:00)
Closing the loop
  • Annotation queues: Compliance failures go to domain experts, and their reviewed traces feed back into the golden dataset; evaluation went from weeks or months to minutes, with a blog post and repo to reproduce (16:00–19:20)

Why Datasite Stopped Building Its Own Parsing - Adit Abraham, Reducto & Raj Bakhru, Datasite

AI Engineer · Adit Abraham (Reducto co-founder and CEO) with Raj Bakhru (Datasite global CTO), fireside chat · uploaded 4 Nov, 2:30 PM IST · 19:49
Verdict: skim from 5:00 — good real-world buy-versus-build numbers, though it's partly a Reducto customer story.
🔁 Repeat: 'AI for finance' — seen 22 times before, 'document parsing' 4 times and 'RAG pipelines' 4 times. New here: Datasite's scale and its buy-versus-build reasoning, plus a 22% recall gain it credits to Reducto parsing.
Datasite's scale
  • Where M&A data lives: About 60% of transactions over $100 million run through Datasite's virtual data rooms, with over a trillion tokens a year, 500,000+ monthly users, 55,000 data rooms a year and billions of pages (0:00–4:00)
  • Why it bought Blue Flame: Bakhru's M&A AI startup was a week from closing a VC round when Datasite, asked to put in $5 million, offered over dinner to buy the whole company; competitors had raised hundreds of millions, and data-room content plus Grata's 20 million private-company records gave proprietary data (8:00–12:00)
Why they stopped building parsing
  • Everything is an edge case: Errors came from a pie-chart legend's shade of blue (the same data as a bar chart was read correctly), merged cells in finance spreadsheets, and XLSB or macro files; support time on these was huge (5:00–8:00)
  • Spend engineers on revenue: With about 250 engineers and a goal of ~20% growth (about $200 million new revenue a year), he wants them on five revenue projects, not solved layers; he sees a "barbell" where senior and very junior engineers gain most from AI coding (13:00–16:00)
Pipeline and results
  • The stack: Reducto runs inside Datasite's environment, handling bursts of up to a terabyte, then content goes to Turbopuffer as the vector database with graph RAG queries in Python on top (17:00–18:00)
  • VDR Bench: A private benchmark of a few hundred questions over a data room of hundreds of documents, run on every change, which must beat dropping the same files into Claude or OpenAI projects; they credit Reducto parsing with 22% better recall and say most of the work is on extraction, not answering (18:00–19:49)
  • Remaining friction: Getting cheaper models to stop tool-call spamming so they can move off pricier ones, while always optimizing quality first, then cost and speed (16:00–17:00)

Building the AI Agent Is Easy. Proving It's Safe Isn't - Jonathan Osborne, NatWest

AI Engineer · Jonathan Osborne, NatWest Group (lead AI engineer) · uploaded 4 Nov, 1:30 PM IST · 18:01
Verdict: worth watching from 7:00 — the judge-calibration and human-agreement numbers are rare production data, even though the theme is familiar.
🔁 Repeat: 'evals for agents' — seen 54 times before, 'agent observability' 30 times, 'human-in-the-loop review' 29 times, 'AI in regulated industries' 23 times and 'LLM-as-a-judge' 11 times. New here: real bank numbers, including a judge with almost zero correlation to humans, humans agreeing only 64% on intent, and a 43% pass rate on topic changes.
Context and risk
  • Scale: NatWest has about 19 million UK retail customers, and its assistant Cora handles about 100,000 conversations a day, mostly scripted; about a third of journeys are moving to RAG answers and agents that can make payments or change addresses (0:00–2:00)
  • Four new failure modes: Confidently wrong, talked into things via prompt injection, right but unhelpful, and non-deterministic, so years of scripted regression tests no longer apply (3:00–5:00)
Shrink first, then measure
  • Routing funnel: A context filter removes about 20% of events like button clicks, deterministic rules route more, then a gated rephrasing step, a neural intent classifier over embeddings (not generative) and policy rules, all versioned config for audit and fallback (5:00–7:00)
  • Quality as a vector: Scores split into safety, task and experience; a 94%-accurate router that's 100% confident when wrong is worse than an 85% one that knows its limits (6:00–8:00)
  • Human validation: 111 payment-agent conversations over 37 journeys and 4 brands with 3 scorers each passed 83% overall, but 97% on happy paths, 76% on edge cases and only 43% on topic changes; humans agreed 95% on bias but only 64% on intent understanding (7:00–10:00)
LLM judges in production
  • Generous judges are useless: On about 14,000 complaint cases scored by humans and a judge, the judge averaged 0.97 against humans' 0.83 with almost no correlation; swapping only the judge model dropped scores sharply, so the judge model is part of the config, and they moved it to a different model family than the agent (9:00–12:00)
  • Pipeline: OpenTelemetry/OpenInference traces, a 5% random sample plus every handoff, failure, long or angry chat, scoring every 4 hours, a hard CI/CD regression gate, Comet Opik for the agent and MPM for the classifier's drift, all air-gapped with no PII leaving (11:00–15:00)
  • Rollout: Shadow mode first (classify live traffic while scripts still answer), then brand-by-brand percentage rollout with feature flags that revert in seconds; complaint handlers must give feedback before rerunning an investigation (14:00–18:01)
